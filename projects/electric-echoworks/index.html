What I built

Electric EchoWorks grew from individual signal-processing tools into a desktop suite and a React/TypeScript web interface backed by Python processors. It brings related audio experiments into a shared workflow with listening previews, visual comparisons, exports, and saved jobs.

My contribution

Developed the application workflows and integration around classical processing and pretrained models, including background jobs, preview generation, storage/history, and a shared web processing service. Related Voice Coach work explores speaking-volume, pace, and target-word feedback.

  • Audio conversion, trimming, and video-audio extraction.
  • Quiet Labs denoising and Dog Ears frequency-range exploration.
  • Signal Studio frequency shifting and bounded multiplexing experiments.
  • Demucs-based vocal separation and English/Arabic Meeting Notes.

Components

Desktop & web suite

Audio Converter, Quiet Labs, Dog Ears, Signal Studio, Vocal Separator, and Meeting Notes.

Voice Coach

Related speaking-volume, estimated pace, and confirmed target-word feedback.

Earlier QuietLab prototype

Noise-profile selection, STFT processing, adjustable reduction, waveform/spectrum comparison, and WAV export.

Design choices

  • Make before/after listening central to evaluating processing.
  • Use saved, bounded jobs so navigating away does not cancel computation.
  • Keep audio-tool history distinct from temporary, consented Voice Coach speech requests.

Scope & results

The public sample illustrates frequency filtering with generated audio. Full transcription and source separation require the local Python service and pretrained models. Their quality depends on the recording; model training from scratch is not claimed.
