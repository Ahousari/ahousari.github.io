What I built

ECE 4310 Lab 2 became an end-to-end experiment in building and evaluating a six-class pedestrian device-use detector. The work included personal and supplemental footage, dataset releases, label review, model-size comparisons, GPU training, and explicit examination of generalization.

My contribution

Developed the dataset preparation and review workflows, split checks, Palmetto training helpers, model-comparison tools, and evaluation/reporting pipeline. Experiments compared clean-filtered and ambiguity-inclusive training sets while retaining the validation and historical test partitions.

  • Six categories of visible phone/headphone behavior.
  • Multiple dataset releases and eight completed V4/V4.1 model variants.
  • Person-disjoint partitions, with documented source-video overlap limitations.
  • Separate geometric detection and correct-class metrics, including confusion analysis.

Components

Data tools

Dataset Viewer, Label Review, progress pages, label audits, and split/provenance checks.

Training & evaluation

Palmetto helpers, saved model comparisons, confusion matrices, and error reports.

Design choices

  • Track data provenance and distinguish personal footage from supplemental sources.
  • Compare macro class performance with aggregate accuracy.
  • Report poor transfer to the historical test rather than promote validation alone.

Scope & results

V4 small scored 244/384 (63.5%) correct-class detections on validation and 54/257 (21.0%) on the reused historical test. V4.1 nano scored 197/384 (51.3%) and 49/257 (19.1%). These are limited experimental results, not deployment accuracy.
