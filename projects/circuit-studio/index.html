What I built

CircuitStudio brings the steps of an electronics lab together: choose a circuit, change component values, calculate its expected behavior, run a simulation, and inspect the results. Its library covers analog electronics and common circuits from ECE 3110 and ECE 3120.

My contribution

Developed the local application, circuit definitions, analytical explanations, simulation workflow, interactive plots, and export tools. The interface connects the visible circuit to its electrical definition and makes individual runs inspectable through netlists, raw samples, and simulator logs.

  • 49 documented circuit pages and 203 named configurations.
  • DC operating point, transient, AC, and DC-sweep analysis.
  • Probe selection, variation comparisons, and schematic/graph exports.
  • Report preparation with calculation figures, tables, and provenance manifests.

Components

Circuit library

Diodes, transistors, amplifiers, filters, oscillators, and named component variations.

Measurement & reporting

Interactive traces, calculations, result tables, and reusable figure exports.

Design choices

  • Keep hand calculations and simulator results separate so their differences remain visible.
  • Reuse unchanged, saved SPICE runs while solving modified circuits again.
  • Package the interface for Windows while retaining downloadable numerical results.

Scope & results

The full application runs locally with LTspice and educational semiconductor models. This browser workbench calculates an unloaded voltage divider and an ideal RC low-pass response analytically; it is not an online SPICE solver.
